40k factions in depth




  40k factions in depth
  One entry per faction, written for the box's Encyclopedia screen: who they are, how they
  play, the idea their rules are built on, the units you will see across the table, and one tip for
  facing them.

  The "signature idea" names come from each faction's most recent full rules. The 11th
  edition Faction Packs (June 2026) kept most armies playing the way they did, but exact
  wording and names can change with each codex, so the app or the Faction Pack is the
  authority. No stats are given here.


  Imperium

  Space Marines
          Who: Genetically engineered super-soldiers in power armour, organised into
          Chapters of a thousand. The Emperor's angels of death.
          How they play: Small, tough, flexible units that are good at everything and best at
          nothing.
          Signature idea: Oath of Moment. Each turn they single out one enemy unit, and the
          whole army is more accurate against it.
          You will see: Intercessors, Terminators, Dreadnoughts, Gladiator and Land Raider
          tanks.
          Against them: Their marked target is going to die. Give them a choice between two
          things you can afford to lose.


  Blood Angels, Dark Angels, Space Wolves, Black Templars, Deathwatch
          Who: Space Marine Chapters with their own units and character. Blood Angels are
          noble and cursed with a thirst for blood. Dark Angels guard an old secret. Space
          Wolves are sagas and fangs. Black Templars are crusading zealots. Deathwatch are
          picked alien-hunters from every Chapter.
          How they play: Space Marines with a lean. Blood Angels and Space Wolves want
          melee, Dark Angels refuse to move, Black Templars charge in mobs, Deathwatch bring
          a tool for every target.
          You will see: Death Company and Sanguinary Guard; Deathwing Knights and
          Ravenwing bikes; Thunderwolf Cavalry; Crusader Squads; mixed Deathwatch
          Veterans.

          Against them: Shoot the melee Chapters before they arrive. Out-score the stubborn
          ones.


  Astra Militarum
          Who: The Imperial Guard. Ordinary men and women, in their billions, with tanks.
          How they play: Lots of cheap infantry holding ground while artillery and battle tanks
          do the killing.
          Signature idea: Orders. Officers shout commands that make nearby squads shoot,
          move or hold better.
          You will see: Cadian Shock Troops, Leman Russ tanks, Basilisks, Rogal Dorns, Kasrkin.
          Against them: Kill the officers and reach the guns. Guardsmen fold in melee.


  Adeptus Custodes
          Who: The Emperor's own bodyguard. Each one is worth a squad of Space Marines.
          How they play: A dozen or so nearly unkillable heroes. Devastating in melee.
          Signature idea: Martial stances chosen as they fight, trading extra hits for precision.
          You will see: Custodian Guard, Wardens, Allarus Terminators, jetbikes, Sisters of
          Silence.
          Against them: There are very few of them. Spread out and score where they are not.


  Adepta Sororitas
          Who: The Sisters of Battle. Warrior nuns with bolters, flamers and absolute faith.
          How they play: Mid-range firefights at close quarters, with units that get more
          dangerous as they take losses.
          Signature idea: Miracle dice. They bank dice during the game and swap one in when a
          roll matters.
          You will see: Battle Sisters, Seraphim, Repentia, Penitent Engines, Exorcist tanks.
          Against them: Expect the important roll to succeed. Plan for it.


  Adeptus Mechanicus
          Who: The machine-cult of Mars. Cyborg priests who worship knowledge and hoard it.
          How they play: Layers of shooting from fragile troops, backed by robots, with many
          small bonuses to track.

          Signature idea: Doctrina Imperatives. Each round the army switches between an
          attacking and a defending mode.
          You will see: Skitarii Rangers and Vanguard, Kastelan Robots, Onager Dunecrawlers,
          Ironstriders.
          Against them: Get close. They are far worse in melee than at range.


  Grey Knights
          Who: A secret Chapter of psychic Space Marines who exist to fight daemons.
          How they play: A small elite army that teleports around the table.
          Signature idea: Teleporting units out and back in to wherever they are needed.
          You will see: Terminators, Strike Squads, Nemesis Dreadknights.
          Against them: Guard your back objectives. They will appear there.


  Imperial Agents
          Who: Inquisitors, assassins, Arbites and Navy crews. The Imperium's specialists.
          How they play: Mostly as a few allied units inside another Imperial army.
          You will see: Inquisitors with retinues, the four Assassins, Exaction Squads.
          Against them: The Assassins hunt characters. Keep yours screened.


  Imperial Knights
          Who: Noble houses piloting walking war machines the height of a building.
          How they play: Three to six huge models and a few smaller Armigers. Every loss is a
          disaster for them.
          Signature idea: Knightly oaths that reward them for honourable deeds.
          You will see: Questoris Knights, Armiger Warglaives and Helverins.
          Against them: Bring weapons made for big targets, and take objectives with bodies;
          they have few.


  Chaos

  Chaos Space Marines
          Who: Marines who turned traitor ten thousand years ago and have been raiding from
          hell ever since.

          How they play: Like Space Marines with a nasty streak, plus cultists and daemon
          engines.
          Signature idea: Dark Pacts. A unit can gamble for a stronger attack, at the risk of
          hurting itself.
          You will see: Legionaries, Chosen, Terminators, Possessed, Forgefiends, Abaddon the
          Despoiler.
          Against them: They are strongest when they take the gamble. Make them take it
          often.


  Death Guard
          Who: The plague legion of Nurgle. Bloated, rotting and cheerful about it.
          How they play: Slow and extremely hard to kill, weakening everything that comes
          near.
          Signature idea: A contagion aura that makes nearby enemies easier to hurt, spreading
          as the game goes on.
          You will see: Plague Marines, Deathshroud Terminators, Poxwalkers, Plagueburst
          Crawlers, Mortarion.
          Against them: Do not fight them at close range if you can help it. They are slow; make
          them walk.


  World Eaters
          Who: Khorne's berserkers. They want blood and do not care whose.
          How they play: Almost no shooting. Everything runs at you.
          Signature idea: Blessings of Khorne. Each round they roll dice and spend them on
          army-wide boosts.
          You will see: Khorne Berzerkers, Eightbound, Jakhals, Angron.
          Against them: Feed them cheap units and shoot what remains.


  Thousand Sons
          Who: Sorcerers of Tzeentch leading armoured suits full of dust.
          How they play: Psychic attacks and strong mid-range shooting; very slow infantry.
          Signature idea: A cabal of sorcerers who pool power to cast rituals each turn.
          You will see: Rubric Marines, Scarab Occult Terminators, Tzaangors, Magnus the Red.
          Against them: Kill the sorcerers. The rest of the army runs on them.

  Emperor's Children
          Who: Slaanesh's legion, chasing perfection and sensation. A full faction since 2025.
          How they play: Fast, elite assault troops with sonic weapons.
          Signature idea: Speed. They advance or fall back and still charge.
          You will see: Noise Marines, Infractors, Tormentors, Fulgrim.
          Against them: You cannot run from them. Screen with cheap units.


  Chaos Daemons
          Who: Creatures of the four Chaos gods made real.
          How they play: Four armies in one: Khorne's melee, Tzeentch's magic, Nurgle's
          toughness, Slaanesh's speed.
          Signature idea: They are stronger inside a zone of warp influence that spreads across
          the table.
          You will see: Bloodletters, Pink Horrors, Plaguebearers, Daemonettes, Greater
          Daemons.
          Against them: Almost everything has a save that ignores your weapon's quality. Use
          volume of attacks.


  Chaos Knights
          Who: Fallen Knight houses.
          How they play: As Imperial Knights, with more small War Dogs and effects that shake
          enemy morale.
          You will see: War Dog Brigands and Karnivores, Knight Abominants, Knight
          Desecrators.
          Against them: The same as Imperial Knights: anti-tank weapons and more bodies on
          objectives.


  Xenos

  Orks
          Who: Green, loud and built for war. They believe they are having a wonderful time, and
          they are.
          How they play: A flood of infantry and ramshackle vehicles heading for melee.

          Signature idea: Waaagh! Once per game the whole army surges: faster, harder to stop,
          deadlier in melee.
          You will see: Boyz, Meganobz, Deff Dreads, Battlewagons, Ghazghkull Thraka.
          Against them: Hold something back for the Waaagh! turn. It is coming on turn two or
          three.


  Tyranids
          Who: An extragalactic swarm that eats worlds.
          How they play: Either carpets of small creatures, a wall of monsters, or both.
          Signature idea: Synapse. Leader creatures hold the swarm together; kill them and it
          falters.
          You will see: Termagants, Hormagaunts, Genestealers, Carnifexes, Hive Tyrants.
          Against them: Shoot the synapse creatures first.


  Necrons
          Who: Ancient machine dynasties waking after sixty million years.
          How they play: Slow, durable, and they get back up. See the tab on your own army.
          Signature idea: Reanimation Protocols.
          Against them: Finish units off. Never leave one model standing.


  T'au Empire
          Who: A young, optimistic alien empire with the best guns in the galaxy.
          How they play: Almost entirely shooting. One unit spots, another fires.
          Signature idea: For the Greater Good. A spotting unit makes a second unit's shooting
          more accurate.
          You will see: Fire Warriors, Crisis and Broadside battlesuits, Riptides, Hammerheads,
          Kroot.
          Against them: Get into melee. They have almost no answer to it.


  Aeldari
          Who: The ancient space elves of the craftworlds, a dying people of great skill.
          Harlequins and the Ynnari fight alongside them.
          How they play: Fast, fragile specialists. Each unit does one thing extremely well.

          Signature idea: Battle Focus. Small extra moves that let them strike and slip away.
          You will see: Guardians, Aspect Warriors, Wraithguard, Wave Serpents, the Avatar of
          Khaine.
          Against them: Anything you catch dies. The hard part is catching it.


  Drukhari
          Who: The Aeldari who never gave up their cruelty. Raiders from the hidden city of
          Commorragh.
          How they play: Very fast, very fragile, riding open-topped skimmers.
          Signature idea: Power from Pain. They gain tokens from suffering and spend them on
          bonuses.
          You will see: Kabalite Warriors, Wyches, Incubi, Raiders, Talos.
          Against them: Shoot the transports. The passengers are delicate.


  Genestealer Cults
          Who: Human workers secretly infected by Tyranids, rising up when the swarm arrives.
          How they play: Ambush. Units appear from underground, strike and vanish, and
          destroyed ones can return.
          Signature idea: Cult Ambush.
          You will see: Neophyte Hybrids, Acolytes, Aberrants, Goliath trucks, the Patriarch.
          Against them: Block the places they want to arrive. Keep units near each other.


  Leagues of Votann
          Who: The Kin. Stocky, practical, clone-born miners and traders. Space dwarfs.
          How they play: Tough infantry and solid vehicles at medium range.
          Signature idea: Grudges. Enemies that harm them are marked, and marked enemies
          are easier to kill.
          You will see: Hearthkyn Warriors, Einhyr Hearthguard, Sagitaurs, the Hekaton Land
          Fortress.
          Against them: Do not give them a grudge target for free.
