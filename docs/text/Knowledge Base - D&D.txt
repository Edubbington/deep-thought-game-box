 Dungeons & Dragons
 What the box needs to know to teach D&D to a table that has never played, and to help
 run a session. It covers the current rules: fifth edition as revised in the 2024 core
 rulebooks. The basic rules are free on D&D Beyond, and the box explains them in its own
 words.



 What D&D is
 One player is the Dungeon Master (DM). The DM describes the world and plays everyone
 in it. Each other player runs one character. There is no board and no winner. The game is a
 conversation:

      1. The DM describes the situation.
      2. The players say what their characters do.
      3. When the outcome is uncertain, someone rolls a twenty-sided die.
      4. The DM says what happens, and the loop starts again.


 The one roll that runs the game
 Roll a d20, add a modifier, and try to equal or beat a target number. That is called a D20
 Test, and it comes in three kinds.

      throw         spell, poison


 The modifier is the bonus from one of your six abilities, plus your Proficiency Bonus if you
 are trained in what you are doing. The Proficiency Bonus starts at +2 and rises with level.

 The six abilities: Strength (muscle), Dexterity (agility), Constitution (toughness),
 Intelligence (reasoning), Wisdom (awareness), Charisma (force of personality). A score of
 10 gives +0; every 2 points above or below changes the modifier by 1.

 Advantage and Disadvantage. When things are in your favour, roll two d20s and keep the
 higher. When they are against you, roll two and keep the lower. If you have both, they
 cancel and you roll one.

 Heroic Inspiration. A reward the DM hands out. Spend it to re-roll any one die.

 A natural 20 on an attack always hits and is a critical hit: roll the weapon's damage dice
 twice. A natural 1 on an attack always misses.


 Building a character, in four choices
      1. Class: what you do. It matters most.
      2. Background: what you were before. It gives ability score increases, skills and a
        starting feat.
      3. Species: what you are. It gives a few traits.
      4. Details: name, look, personality.

 Species in the 2024 rules: Aasimar, Dragonborn, Dwarf, Elf, Gnome, Goliath, Halfling,
 Human, Orc, Tiefling.

 Characters run from level 1 to level 20. You pick a subclass, a specialism within your class,
 at level 3.


 Combat
 Combat runs in rounds. A round is about six seconds, and everyone gets one turn in it.

 Start: everyone rolls Initiative (a d20 plus Dexterity modifier). Highest goes first. That
 order holds for the whole fight.

 On your turn you get:

        Movement: up to your Speed, usually 30 feet. You can split it before and after your
        action.
        One action.
        One bonus action, only if something you have says it uses one.
        One free interaction with an object: draw a sword, open a door.

 Once per round, at any time: one reaction. The common one is the Opportunity Attack, a
 free swing at an enemy who walks out of your reach.

 Hitting and hurting. Roll a d20 plus your attack bonus. If it equals or beats the target's AC,
 roll your weapon's damage and subtract it from the target's Hit Points.

 At 0 Hit Points a character falls Unconscious and starts making Death Saving Throws at
 the start of each turn: a plain d20, 10 or higher succeeds. Three successes and you are
 stable. Three failures and the character dies. A natural 20 brings you back with 1 Hit Point.
 A natural 1 counts as two failures. Any healing ends the whole business and wakes you up.



 Resting
        Short Rest: about an hour. Spend Hit Dice to heal, and some abilities come back.
        Long Rest: about eight hours. All Hit Points and almost everything else comes back.


 Magic in five rules
      1. Cantrips are small spells you can cast as often as you like.
      2. Bigger spells use spell slots. A slot is spent when you cast and comes back after a
        Long Rest.
      3. A spell cast in a higher slot is often stronger.
      4. Concentration: some spells last only while you concentrate. You can concentrate on
        one at a time, and taking damage forces a Constitution save to keep it (DC 10 or half
        the damage, whichever is higher).
      5. You can spend only one spell slot per turn.

 What the box does at a D&D table
        Initiative tracker. Enter names and rolls; the screen shows whose turn it is and who is
        next. The button means "next turn".
        Round counter and timers for effects that last a set number of rounds.
        Condition tags on each name, with a one-line reminder when that creature's turn
        starts.
        Concentration marker, with the save DC worked out when damage is entered.
        Death save tracker: three boxes each way.
        Dice: any die, with Advantage and Disadvantage built in.
        Rules lookup: hold the button and ask "what does Restrained do".
        DM helpers: a random name, a quick shopkeeper, a difficulty class suggestion.
        The voice. Deep Thought makes a patient, faintly weary DM's assistant. It announces
        turns and never tells the DM what to rule.

 It does not hold character sheets or monster statistics. Those stay on paper or on D&D
 Beyond.


 A first session, for a table of beginners
      1. One person agrees to be DM. Everyone else picks from Fighter, Rogue, Cleric and
         Wizard. The current starter set, Heroes of the Borderlands, is built around exactly
        those four.
      2. The box explains the d20 roll with one example each of a check, an attack and a save.
      3. Play a short scene with no fighting: arrive somewhere, talk to someone, make two or
        three checks.
      4. First fight, three or four weak enemies. The box runs initiative and explains movement,
        action and bonus action the first time each comes up.
      5. Take a Short Rest and let the box explain healing.
      6. Stop after about two hours, on a question.


 Words a new player will hear
 AC: Armour Class, how hard you are to hit. DC: Difficulty Class, the number to beat. HP:
 Hit Points. d20, d8, 2d6: dice; 2d6 means two six-sided dice. Modifier: the number you
 add. Proficiency: being trained. Saving throw: a roll to resist. Cantrip: a free spell. Spell
