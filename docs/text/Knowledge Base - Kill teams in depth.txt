Kill teams in depth




  Kill teams in depth
  The roster table in the main tab says who each team is and how hard it is. This tab goes
  one level down: how each team wins, the one trick you have to learn to play it, and what to
  do when it is across the table from you.

  Team rules are free downloads and are rebalanced every quarter, so this tab describes
  how a team plays, not its numbers. The team's own rules card is always the authority. Four
  teams from 2026 boxes are covered in less detail because I have not verified their rules:
  Celestian Insidiants, Spectre Squad, Legion of the Damned and Ork Boss Nobz.


  Seven habits that win games with any team
      1. Play the mission. Most points come from objectives and your Tac Op. Killing is one
         third of the score at most.
      2. Count activations. The player with operatives left to activate after the other has run
         out gets free moves. Elite teams always have fewer; plan for it.
      3. Conceal first, Engage when it pays. An operative on a Conceal order behind cover
         cannot be shot from range. Only switch to Engage when you are about to do
         something worth it.
      4. Trade up. Losing a cheap operative to kill or pin an expensive one is a good exchange.
      5. Activate the operative in danger first, or the one that will kill something before it can
         act.
      6. Stay within reach of cover. An operative in the open at the end of a turning point is
         usually dead in the next.
      7. Save a command point for a re-roll when one dice decides the game.

  Which team for whom
          A first game for two new players: Angels of Death against Plague Marines. Six models
          each, forgiving, and they come in the Starter Set.
          Someone who wants to shoot: Kasrkin, then Pathfinders.
          Someone who wants to charge: Kommandos or Fellgor Ravagers.
          Someone who likes puzzles: Mandrakes, Warpcoven or Void-dancers, after a few
          games.
          Someone who owns Necrons: Hierotek Circle or Canoptek Circle keeps the same
          look and idea on a small board.
