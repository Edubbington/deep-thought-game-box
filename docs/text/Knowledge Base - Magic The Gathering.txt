  Magic: The Gathering
  What the box needs to teach Magic to a new player and to keep track of a game for people
  who already play. Card text always wins over anything here; this tab explains how the
  game works, not what any card does.



  What Magic is
  Each player is a wizard with a deck of cards. You start with 20 life (40 in Commander). You
  play lands to make mana, spend mana to cast spells, and use creatures and other spells to
  bring your opponents' life to zero.

  Ways to lose: your life reaches 0; you have to draw a card and your library is empty; you
  collect ten poison counters; in Commander, one opponent's commander deals you 21
  combat damage over the game.


  The five colours

     Colour            Land       What it is good at

     White             Plains     Many small creatures, protection, life gain, order

     Blue              Island     Drawing cards, countering spells, flying, trickery

     Black             Swamp      Killing creatures, bringing things back from the
                                  graveyard, power at a price

     Red               Mountain   Direct damage, speed, haste, chaos

     Green             Forest     Big creatures, extra mana, growth


  Most decks use one to three colours. More colours means more options and more games
  where you draw the wrong lands.


  Card types
         Land: makes mana. You may play one per turn. Not a spell.
         Creature: attacks and blocks. Has power and toughness, written as 2/3. Stays on the
         battlefield.

         Sorcery: a one-off effect. Only on your own turn, in a main phase.
         Instant: a one-off effect you can cast at almost any time, including on someone else's
         turn.
         Enchantment: a lasting effect that stays on the battlefield.
         Artifact: an object that stays on the battlefield. Equipment is an artifact you attach to
         a creature.
         Planeswalker: an ally with loyalty counters and abilities you use once per turn.
         Opponents can attack it.
         Battle: a card you attack to flip into something better.

  Anything that stays on the battlefield is called a permanent.

  Six rules new players get wrong
     1. Summoning sickness. A creature cannot attack, or use an ability that taps it, on the
        turn it arrives. It can still block.
     2. Tapping. Attacking taps a creature. A tapped creature cannot block. Deciding what to
        hold back is half the game.
     3. Blocking is the defender's choice. The attacker chooses which creatures attack; the
        defender chooses who blocks what.
     4. Damage wears off. A 2/3 that takes 2 damage survives, and is fully healed at end of
        turn.
     5. Instants and abilities use the stack. When someone casts a spell, everyone gets a
        chance to respond. The last thing added happens first.
     6. A countered or destroyed card goes to the graveyard, not back to the hand.


  The stack, in one example
  You cast a spell to give your creature +3/+3. Before it happens, your opponent responds
  with a spell that deals 2 damage to that creature. Their spell was cast last, so it happens
  first, and your creature may die before the boost arrives. If you had another instant, you
  could respond to their response. When everyone passes, the stack empties from the top
  down.

  Commander's extra rules. Your commander starts in the command zone and can be cast
  from there. Each time it has been cast from there before, it costs 2 more. Your deck may
  only contain cards in your commander's colours. 21 combat damage from one commander
  knocks a player out.

  Mulligans. If your opening seven is unplayable, shuffle it back, draw seven again, and put
  one card on the bottom for each mulligan you have taken. In multiplayer games the first
  one is free.


  What the box does at a Magic table
         Life totals for two to four players, in large numbers, with plus and minus buttons and a
         history so a disputed total can be traced.
         Commander damage grid: who has dealt how much to whom, with a warning at 21.
         Counters: poison, energy, experience, and the monarch and initiative markers.
         Turn prompts: the box names each step as the button is pressed. Useful for teaching;
         switched off for experienced tables.
         Who goes first: a roll-off with commentary.
         Keyword lookup: hold the button and ask "what does ward do".
         Dice and coin flips.
         Turn timer for slow four-player games, with a dry remark when someone takes too
         long.

  It does not know what individual cards do and does not judge card interactions. For a real
  dispute, the card text and the official rules decide.

  Teaching a first game
     1. Use two simple decks. The Foundations Beginner Box is made for this: it walks two
         players through a scripted first game and then gives them themed half-decks to mix.
     2. Play the first game with hands face up. The box announces each step.
     3. Explain lands and mana on turn one, creatures and summoning sickness on turn two,
        attacking and blocking on turn three.
     4. Leave instants and the stack until someone holds one and wants to use it.
     5. Play a second game with hands hidden.
     6. For a group of four, move to Commander with ready-made decks. Wizards released a
         set of beginner-oriented Foundations Commander decks in October 2026.


  Words a new player will hear
  Tap: turn a card sideways to show it has been used. Library: your deck. Graveyard: your
  discard pile. Exile: removed from the game. Mana value: the total cost of a card. Token: a
  permanent made by an effect, not a card. Counter (verb): cancel a spell. Counter (noun):
  a marker on a card, such as +1/+1. Removal: a spell that gets rid of a creature. Ramp:
  getting extra mana early. Aggro, control, combo: fast decks, slow decks, and decks that
  win with a specific set of cards.
