Your Necron army




  Your Necron army
  A deep dive on the Necrons for the collection on your shelf: what each unit is for, how the
  army plays in 11th edition, and the lore the box can tell. Unit stats and points are left out on
  purpose; they are on the datasheets and in the Warhammer 40,000 app, and they change
  with every balance update.



  The one rule that makes Necrons Necrons
  Reanimation Protocols. At the end of your Command phase, every Necron unit with this
  ability gets D3 wounds back. Those wounds are spent in order: heal damaged models first,
  then stand destroyed models back up. A unit that has been wiped out completely does
  not come back.

  What that means at the table:

         Your opponent has to finish units off. A squad left on two models is a squad again two
         turns later.
         Big units get more from it than small ones, because there is more to bring back.
         Half-killing three of your units is your opponent's worst result. Encourage it: give
         them several targets, not one.

  You spend Detachment Points (DP) on detachments. Each detachment gives one army-
  wide bonus, a set of stratagems and a choice of Enhancements. Each also sets a Force
  Disposition, which decides what kind of mission objectives you are given.

  Other Necron detachments exist (Starshatter Arsenal, Annihilation Legion, Cursed
  Legion, Cryptek Conclave, Skyshroud Spearhead, The Phaeron's Armoury); they reward
  units you do not have yet.


  Three ways to play your collection
  First game: no Void Dragon, Awakened Dynasty. Overlord with ten Warriors, Royal
  Warden with the other ten, Lychguard, Praetorians, Doomstalker, two Scarab units. One
  rule to remember: units with a character hit better. Ask your opponent to bring a similar
  amount.

  The elite version: Obeisance Phalanx. Overlord with the Lychguard as the hammer,
  Warriors as one block of twenty on the middle objective, Praetorians in reserve. Each
  Command phase, mark the enemy unit you most need dead.

  The big game: add the Void Dragon. At 2,000 points you have three Detachment Points,
  so Pantheon of Woe can sit alongside a one-point detachment. The Void Dragon walks up
  the middle and the rest of the army follows it.


  How Necrons win
     1. Stand on objectives and refuse to die. You score by being there at the right moment,
        not by killing.
     2. Never offer a unit that can be wiped in one go. Keep the Warriors in cover and
         together.
     3. Shoot one target until it is gone. Your opponent does not have Reanimation Protocols.
         Finished units stay finished.
     4. Save the fast pieces. Praetorians and Scarabs win games in the last two rounds by
        taking objectives nobody is guarding.
     5. Protect the characters. Half your bonuses switch off when a leader dies.

  What Necrons are bad at: speed. Most of the army walks. Plan where you want to be on
  turn three before you deploy.

  The lore, for the box to tell
  Who they were. The Necrontyr were a short-lived, bitter people who lost a war against
  the Old Ones, the first masters of the galaxy. Desperate, they made a bargain with the
  C'tan, beings of pure energy that fed on stars.

  Biotransference. The C'tan gave the Necrontyr immortal bodies of living metal. The price
  was everything else. Minds were copied into machines and souls were consumed. Nobles
  kept their personalities; the common people became the near-mindless Warriors.

  The War in Heaven. With their new bodies and the C'tan beside them, the Necrons
  destroyed the Old Ones. Then their ruler, Szarekh the Silent King, turned on the C'tan in
  revenge and shattered them into shards, which the Necrons now keep chained and use as
  weapons.

  The Great Sleep. Exhausted, the Necrons sealed themselves in tomb worlds to wait out
  their remaining enemies. They slept for sixty million years. They are waking now, into a
  galaxy full of younger species living on what they consider their property.

  How they are organised. Dynasties, each ruled by a Phaeron, with Overlords and Lords
  beneath. Crypteks are the technomancers who keep everything running. The Triarch
  Praetorians are the old law-keepers, answerable to the Silent King and not to any one
  dynasty. Lychguard are the nobles' personal guard. Canoptek constructs, the Scarabs and
  Doomstalkers, are the maintenance machines of the tombs.

  What has gone wrong. Sixty million years damaged many of them. Destroyers are
  Necrons whose only remaining thought is ending all life. Flayed Ones carry a curse that
  makes them crave flesh they can no longer use.

  Names worth knowing. Imotekh the Stormlord, the great general of the Sautekh dynasty.
  Szarekh, the Silent King, who has returned. Trazyn the Infinite, who collects people and
  events for his museum. Orikan the Diviner, an astromancer who rewrites time when it
  disagrees with him. Illuminor Szeras, who engineered biotransference and is still
  experimenting.

  Your Void Dragon. The C'tan called the Void Dragon was the war-god of machines, said to
  command metal itself. Its shards are among the most dangerous things a dynasty can
  field. Imperial legend holds that a great fragment lies buried beneath Mars.

  Necrons in Kill Team
  Two Necron kill teams exist: the Hierotek Circle (a Cryptek with Immortals and
  Deathmarks) and the Canoptek Circle (a Cryptek with Canoptek constructs, from the
  Tomb World box). Neither uses the models you own, but both play on the same idea:
  things that get back up.
